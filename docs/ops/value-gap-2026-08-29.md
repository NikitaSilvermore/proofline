# The $797 problem — why it feels like $19.99, and what closes the gap

> **TIER: REFERENCE** — analysis and options, written 2026-08-29 after Nikita
> played with the live ACL build and said: *"there's no way I can justify why it
> would be $797 per month. If I was a coach I'd imagine paying like $19.99."*
> **PRICING.md remains the law.** Nothing here changes a price. This is about
> what has to exist before the existing price is defensible.

---

## 1. The diagnosis: the instinct is correct, and PRICING.md already predicted it

PRICING.md §0 says it outright:

> *"Every P&L claim (refunds defended, renewals armed, testimonials with
> provenance) runs through baseline + check-ins + flags. A tier missing any
> part of that can only be sold as a tracker, and **trackers price at
> £9/month**."*

That is exactly what happened. Nikita looked at the built product and priced it
at £9–20, because **what is built today is the tracking half of a product whose
price is justified by the P&L half.** The pricing logic is not wrong. The
product is simply not finished to the line the pricing describes.

Concretely — promised at Standard ($797) in PRICING.md §1, **not built**:

| Promised at Standard | Status |
|---|---|
| Full stamp-book win-card art system (four treatments) | ⬜ not built |
| Completion-ceremony stamp book | ⬜ not built |
| Day-one time capsule resurfaced at a late milestone | ◐ a closing paragraph, not a ceremony |
| Harvest automation ("ask while it's hot" timing) | ◐ a queue, no automation |
| SMS delivery | ⬜ not live |
| CRM write-back (Close / GHL) | ⬜ not built |
| Custom milestone architecture at onboarding | ✅ **built 2026-08-29** (ACL rail editor) |

And promised at Signature ($2,497): the closer's demo kit — ⬜ not built.

**So the first answer to "how do we justify $797" is unglamorous: build the
thing that was already priced.** No new invention required for a large part of
the gap.

## 2. But there is a deeper gap, and it is not on the feature list

Three structural problems that no amount of polish fixes:

**(a) The product never tells the coach what it is worth.** It shows the coach
their athletes. It never shows them *its own contribution*. A tool that reports
its own ROI is worth several times a tool that does not, because the buyer
never has to do the arithmetic — or remember to. PRICING.md §6 puts an ROI
calculator on the *pricing page*; it belongs *inside the console*, running on
real data, every month, forever.

**(b) Nothing leaves the building.** Coaches pay for outputs. Today the only
export is a plain-text case-study draft behind a modal. There is no artifact a
coach can post, send to a prospect, or show a partner. The stamp-book win cards
were designed for exactly this and were never built.

**(c) The coach looks at it; they do not work in it.** Software becomes
expensive when it is where the job happens. Right now the console is a beautiful
read-only report: it says "Marcus has missed three check-ins" and then the coach
leaves the app to do something about it. Nothing is captured, nothing is
tracked, nothing accrues. Switching cost is near zero because there is nothing
to switch away from.

## 3. Three pillars that convert a tracker into a $797 instrument

### Pillar A — The retention ledger *(highest value per unit of build)*

Make the product prove its own worth, in the buyer's currency.

- **Intervention tracking.** When a coach nudges a red, record it. When that
  athlete recovers to green, record that too, and attribute it.
- **The saves panel, at the top of the console.** *"This quarter: 14 drifts
  flagged · 11 acted on · 8 recovered · £X of programme value retained."*
  For NLS that sentence is denominated in refunds and clawback — the exact
  language already in Ted's ROI paragraph.
- **Refund-window register.** The most expensive event NLS has is a $10k refund
  inside the window. Flag anyone unengaged *inside their window*, loudly, as a
  distinct thing from ordinary amber.
- **Escalation ladder.** A red untouched for seven days escalates to the
  programme owner. That is the difference between a dashboard and a system.

*Why this is first:* it is the only feature that answers the buyer's actual
question. Everything else is an argument; this is a receipt.

### Pillar B — The proof factory *(the easiest value to see)*

Turn journeys into assets the coach would otherwise pay an agency for.

- **The win card.** Already designed, never built. Every `milestone_earned`
  produces a branded, consented, downloadable image with the real figure on it.
- **Testimonial capture at the peak.** The moment a milestone strikes is the
  moment to ask. Auto-request, capture text and video, store consent, tag with
  the verification level the schema already carries.
- **The verifiable public page.** Opt-in, per athlete: a permanent URL showing
  a verified journey. Third-party-checkable proof is worth an order of
  magnitude more than a screenshot, and it is the only asset here a competitor
  cannot copy by building the same forms.
- **The "Verified" standard.** Already drafted
  (`docs/ops/milestamp-verified-standard-DRAFT.md`). Turning it on makes
  Milestamp the certifying body rather than a vendor. That is a moat, a
  marketing engine, and a pricing floor at once.
- **Monthly asset pack.** N ready-to-post carousels drawn from that programme's
  own consented wins. The posting kit already exists; wire it to real data.

### Pillar C — Make the console the place the work happens

- **Written interventions.** Each red arrives with the message already drafted
  *for that specific reason*, ready to send. The coach's job becomes reading and
  clicking, not composing.
- **Notes and timeline per athlete.** Anything the team knows lives here.
  Instantly creates the data gravity that makes leaving painful.
- **Team seats and roles.** Cassie works the queue; Ted sees the roll-up.
- **Integrations.** Close write-back is already promised. For ACL, TrueCoach and
  Stripe. Every integration is a switching cost.
- **The weekly digest.** A Monday email that makes the console unmissable
  without requiring a login.

## 4. Levers that raise *perceived* worth independent of features

- **It reports its own ROI** (Pillar A) — the single biggest one.
- **It produces artifacts** (Pillar B) — outputs feel expensive; dashboards do not.
- **It is configured, not signed-up-for.** The rail editor now makes onboarding
  a bespoke act: the coach's programme, their words, their milestones. Charge
  for the setup session; make it feel commissioned.
- **It carries a standard.** "Milestamp Verified" is a name, not a feature.
- **It knows things nobody else does.** Once several programmes are on the rail,
  benchmarks ("your week-12 cohort vs all ACL programmes") are unbuyable
  elsewhere.
- **There is a person behind it** — already correctly placed in Signature.
- **Scarcity is real** — ten founding partners, then gone. Already in the plan.

## 5. What I would build, in order

1. **Intervention tracking + the saves panel.** The ROI receipt. Small build,
   largest change in perceived worth.
2. **The win card.** The first artifact that leaves the building.
3. **Drafted interventions + notes.** Turns the console into a workspace.
4. **Refund-window register** (NLS-specific, highest-dollar single event).
5. **Testimonial capture at the peak** + consent/verification surfacing.
6. **CRM write-back**, then the verifiable public page, then benchmarks.

## 6. The honest counter-arguments

- **Do not add features to justify a price.** Feature count does not move price;
  attributable money does. A tracker with forty features is still a tracker.
  Pillars A and B are worth more than everything in §5 items 6+ combined.
- **The founding rate has already solved this problem for a year.** NLS and ACL
  pay **$397**, list $797 stated in the contract. Nobody has to defend $797 to
  anyone yet — the job is to have built the receipt before the first renewal
  conversation, not before the first signature.
- **$797 is right for an NLS-shaped operator and wrong for a small coach — and
  that is deliberate.** At NLS's October target it is 8 basis points of cash.
  For a coach with ten clients it is absurd, which is precisely why Core exists
  as the catch-net and why the target buyer is a programme owner, not a solo
  coach. The instinct "if I were a coach I'd pay $19.99" is the instinct of the
  *wrong buyer* — worth checking who the pitch is actually aimed at.
- **Per-student pricing stays rejected** (PRICING.md §0: it rewards taking
  people off the rail, which is the one thing the product cannot allow). The
  underlying instinct — "price should track the value per athlete" — is better
  served by the saves ledger making the per-athlete value *visible* at a flat
  fee.
- **If, after Pillars A and B exist, it still feels like $19.99 — that is real
  data and the price should move.** Firm the figures at the day-90 review as
  PRICING.md already intends, not before.
